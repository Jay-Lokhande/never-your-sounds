02 / ARRANGEMENT MATH

From bars to seconds.

Plan a loop, a cue, or a whole track at a constant tempo.

16 seconds

8 bars × 4 beats × 0.5 seconds per beat.

“Beat” here means the note named by the denominator. In 6/8, a beat is an eighth note; a dotted-quarter pulse spans three of them. Fractions of beats are supported.

Your studio reference.

EDUCATIONAL REFERENCE

Plain-language reminders for the session. These references do not measure or process an audio file.

Follow the signal

  1. SourceVoice, instrument, or sample
  2. Input & gainSet a clean recording level
  3. Track processingEdit, EQ, dynamics, and effects
  4. Bus & masterCombine and check your mix
  5. ExportRender to the delivery format

A common recording path, not a fixed rule. Sends can route a copy to an effect; buses combine signals. Track your DAW's actual routing.

Speak the language

Gain staging
Managing level through each stage so a signal has useful headroom and avoids unwanted overload.
EQ
Changing the balance of frequency ranges. Try subtracting a problem before boosting everything else.
Compression
Reducing dynamic range according to threshold, ratio, attack, and release settings.
Latency
The delay between an input and its output. Audio buffering and processing can add to it.
Headroom
The space between the current signal level and a chosen limit, such as clipping.

Loudness & export

LUFS
A loudness measure. Integrated loudness describes a programme over time; it is different from the highest peak.
dBTP
True-peak level estimates peaks in the reconstructed waveform, including peaks between samples.
Delivery format
Match the destination's requested format, sample rate, bit depth, and channel layout. Keep a lossless master.
Final listen
Reimport the exported file and listen through it. Check the correct version, tails, channel layout, and any unintended clipping.

There is no single loudness target for every release. For example, EBU R 128 ↗ is a broadcast recommendation, not a universal music mastering preset. Use a suitable meter and your destination's current delivery specification.
